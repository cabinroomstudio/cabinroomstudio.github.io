Privacy Policy

TaqtiqLast updated 20 November 2026

This policy explains what Taqtiq collects, how it's used, and what stays entirely on your device. Taqtiq is developed by Cabin Room Studio.

The short version

Taqtiq has no accounts and never asks for your name, email, or phone number. Your puzzle progress lives on your device. The app does show ads and does report crashes, and those services collect device-level identifiers - the sections below say exactly what and why.

What stays on your device

Your puzzle attempts, ratings, stats, streaks, achievements, campaign progress, Play vs Computer results, spaced-repetition schedule, and all your settings (piece style, board colours, theme, sound) are stored locally on your device. This is not uploaded to us - we operate no server and have no account system, so there is nowhere for it to go.

Android's own Auto Backup may copy your progress and settings to your Google Drive if you have device backup switched on. That backup is controlled by you in Android's settings, is tied to your Google account, and is not readable by us. You can also export a copy yourself from More › Backup & Restore, which writes a JSON file to your Downloads folder or to a place you pick, and only you control it.

Advertising

The app shows ads through Google AdMob. To serve and measure ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects device and app information including your device's advertising ID, IP address, and ad interactions such as impressions and clicks. This is used for ad delivery, frequency capping, and fraud prevention, and may be used for personalised advertising depending on your own settings.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in Android Settings › Privacy › Ads, which also lets you opt out of ad personalisation. Google's handling of this data is covered by the Google Privacy Policy and the How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services page.

In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, the app asks for your consent before any ads are requested, using Google's consent form, the first time you open it. If you do not consent, you still see ads, but they are not personalised. You can change your choice at any time in More › Privacy choices.

If you have Pro, or bought Remove Ads before Pro existed, the app stops requesting and showing ads.

Crash reporting and configuration

The app uses Firebase Crashlytics so that crashes get fixed. When the app crashes it sends a stack trace along with device state - model, Android version, available memory, and a randomly generated installation identifier. That identifier is not linked to you personally and we cannot use it to contact you.

Firebase Remote Config is used to change settings such as the ad frequency without shipping an app update. It fetches configuration values and sends an app instance identifier to do so.

Both are Google services, covered by the Google Privacy Policy.

Purchases

Pro (and the older Remove Ads) is sold through Google Play Billing as a one-time purchase. The purchase itself is handled entirely by Google Play - the app never sees your payment details. It only receives whether the entitlement is active, and stores that answer on your device.

Network requests the app makes

  • Sending a friend invite creates a game challenge on lichess.org.
  • The Analysis screen can fetch a cached engine evaluation from lichess.org for the position you are viewing.
  • In Analysis, positions with seven pieces or fewer can be looked up in the endgame tablebase at tablebase.lichess.ovh.
  • The Live tab fetches tournament broadcasts, games, player profiles and rankings from lichess.org and the public chess.com API (api.chess.com), and chess news from the public feeds of fide.com, chess.com, lichess.org and en.chessbase.com. Pictures shown with events, news stories and players load from the sites that publish them.

These send only board positions, moves and the tournaments, games or players you look up, never anything identifying you. The Live card on Home asks lichess.org which broadcasts are on. Lichess's and chess.com's own privacy policies cover what they do with those requests.

Photographs in History of Chess and Player Biographies are bundled with the app, so viewing them makes no network request.

As with any internet request, the services above receive your IP address.

Children

This app is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them.

Puzzle and artwork sources

Puzzles come from the Lichess open puzzle database (CC0, public domain) and are bundled with the app - solving puzzles needs no network access at all. Piece artwork is bundled from several open-source Lichess piece sets, and the Computer opponent is Stockfish, used under the GPLv3. Pro's analysis also runs Stockfish on your phone, so the positions it analyses never leave the device. Full credits are in More › Info & Credits.

Changes to this policy

If the app changes how data is handled, this policy and the app's Play Store Data Safety listing will be updated to match before the change ships.
